Jobs

A job is a rule attached to a conversation. It is not a session and holds no history of its own.

When the user says "tell me when the deploy finishes", the agent creates a job. The job runs inside the conversation where it was created, so its results appear where the request was made and its context is already present without being restated.

Shape

FieldMeaning
session_idThe conversation this job runs in. Required.
scheduleAn interval, a cron expression, or a single absolute time.
checkOptional. A shell command. Exit status zero means the condition is met.
promptWhat the agent is asked when the job runs.
expires_atWhen the job stops. Required. Defaults to 24 hours after creation.
on_condition_metdelete or continue.

A job with a check costs nothing until it fires. A job without one calls the model on every tick, because deciding whether the condition holds is itself a judgement.

Most conditions are not judgements. "Has the deploy finished" is an exit code. Only questions like "has this review turned hostile" need the model on every tick.

A job runs on its session's model, in its session's context, like any other turn. Nothing about a job is configured separately. That keeps the cost of a check-less job proportional to the conversation it lives in, which is what the rotation threshold already bounds.

It also makes the difference between the two kinds stark, so the agent is instructed to reach for a check first and to fall back to judgement only when no command could decide the question.

Acceptance criteria

  • The agent attempts to express every condition as a check before creating a check-less job.
  • A check-less job records, in the conversation, why no command could decide the condition.
  • The interface marks check-less jobs distinctly and shows their cost per tick.

Acceptance criteria

  • A job with a check consumes no model tokens on a tick where the command exits non-zero.
  • Every tick writes a transcript entry, whether or not the check passed.
  • A job without a check is rejected if its interval is shorter than fifteen minutes.
  • A job whose session is mid-turn defers to the next tick rather than queueing repeats.
  • A job created without an explicit expiry receives one 24 hours out.
  • A message produced by a job is attributed to that job in the interface.

Firing

A job with a check fires when its command exits zero. A job without one fires when the model calls the notify tool during its run.

Calling notify is therefore the condition, the notification, and the record, all at once. A run that does not call it produces no message: the turn is written as an event entry with status not_fired, which is what lets the projection collapse repeated quiet ticks.

Why reuse notify. A judgement poll needs a way to say "yes, now" that is visible and does not depend on parsing prose. The agent already has one, and reusing it means the decision to interrupt and the decision that the condition was met are the same auditable act.

Acceptance criteria

  • A run of a check-less job that does not call notify produces an event entry and no message.
  • A run that calls notify produces a message attributed to the job, and an interrupting notification.
  • Every tick entry carries a status of fired or not_fired.
  • A job set to delete on firing is deleted after the run in which notify was called.

Ending

A job ends by satisfying its condition, by expiring, or by being deleted. Only the first is a success. The other two produce a dead letter.

Dead letters

A job that expires without firing has not failed in any technical sense, yet the thing the user asked about never happened. Deleting it silently loses the request. It becomes a dead letter instead.

FieldMeaning
job_specThe complete job definition, so it can be recreated exactly.
reasonexpired, error, or unavailable.
detailLast error, if any.
run_countHow many times the job ran before dying.
statusopen or closed.

A dead letter notifies immediately rather than waiting to be discovered. Someone waiting to hear about a deploy should learn that the watch gave up, at the moment it gives up.

Acceptance criteria

  • Expiry without firing creates a dead letter and an interrupting notification.
  • A dead letter can be replayed into an identical job with a fresh expiry, in one action.
  • A job failing repeatedly produces one open dead letter, not one per failure.
  • Replaying or dismissing a dead letter closes it rather than deleting it. A replay is visible as the new job it creates.

Failure handling

FailureResponse
Transient — timeout, 5xx, rate limitRetry with exponential backoff, three attempts, then dead letter.
Credit exhausted, authentication, model withdrawnNo retry. Trip the circuit breaker.
Tool error inside a runNot a job failure. The result is returned to the model, which decides what to do.
Check command exits non-zeroThe condition is not met. Not a failure.

Circuit breaker

An exhausted account fails every job at once. Without a breaker, one billing lapse produces dozens of dead letters and dozens of notifications describing the same problem.

Acceptance criteria

  • Failures across three or more distinct jobs within one tick window pause the scheduler.
  • A paused scheduler emits exactly one notification, naming the cause.
  • Jobs failing while the breaker is open do not become dead letters.
  • While the breaker is open, one held job is retried every five minutes as a probe.
  • A successful probe closes the breaker, resumes the schedule, and says so.
  • The breaker state is visible in the interface.

Management

The agent creates, lists, edits, and deletes jobs through a scheduler tool, so it can clean up after itself when the user changes their mind. The same operations are available in the interface.

Acceptance criteria

  • Every job is visible in the interface with its schedule, next run, and expiry.
  • A job can be edited or deleted from the interface without involving the agent.
  • Deleting a session deletes its jobs and its dead letters, because replay requires that session.
  • When a session rotates, its jobs move to the successor.
  • Replaying a dead letter attaches the job to the live session of the chain, not to an archived predecessor.
