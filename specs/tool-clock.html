clock

The current date and time, in UTC and in the operator's zone.

Why it exists

The system prompt is written once and then frozen until a compaction (Sessions). A time written into it would be the time the session was created, and would go on being wrong for as long as the conversation lasted. An agent that schedules work cannot read the clock from its own prompt, so it asks.

What it returns

utc:      2026-09-11T14:32:05Z
local:    2026-09-11T16:32:05+0200 (CEST)
weekday:  Friday 11 September 2026
unix:     1789058825

Four forms because four different questions are asked of a clock: an instant that cannot be misread, the operator's own wall clock, the day of the week — which no timestamp answers without arithmetic — and a number to compute with.

ParameterMeaning
offsetA signed duration from now, such as 90m, 2h30m, -1h, 3d. Optional; absent means now.
Days and weeks are part of the grammar. Go's own duration parser stops at hours, and a job is far more often days away than nanoseconds. s, m, h, d, and w are all accepted, and a malformed offset is refused with an example rather than silently read as zero.

Acceptance criteria

  • The answer carries the instant in UTC, the same instant in the local zone with its name, the weekday, and the Unix seconds.
  • An absent offset answers for now.
  • An offset shifts every one of the four forms by the same amount.
  • A negative offset answers in the past.
  • Units of seconds, minutes, hours, days, and weeks are accepted, and compound forms such as 1h30m parse.
  • A malformed offset fails, quotes what was given, and shows the form expected.
  • A malformed offset is never read as zero, so a bad parse cannot silently answer for now.
