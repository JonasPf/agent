schedule

Create, list, edit, and delete the jobs that make future work visible.

What it does

The write side of Jobs. A job wakes on a schedule and runs a prompt inside the session it belongs to; an optional check decides whether a wake acts.

ActionDoes
createCreates a job on this session unless another is named. Returns when it next wakes and what it will cost.
listEvery job, with its schedule, its next wake, and its recent runs.
editChanges the fields given and leaves the rest. Requires id.
deleteRemoves a job. Defaults to the job the current wake belongs to, so a job can end itself.

Time or condition — the decision the description forces

The hardest thing about this tool is not its parameters. It is that two different requests reach it looking alike, and the wrong reading produces a job that either never fires or burns money on every tick.

The request namesBecomesBecause
A time — "at nine", "in five minutes"A schedule resolving to one instant, and no check.It comes due once and fires. A check that waits for a time to arrive is tested repeatedly, must answer the moment it runs, and will be killed for sleeping.
A condition — "when the build passes"A repeating schedule with a check command.A check costs nothing until it fires. A repeating job without one calls the model on every single tick.

Cost is reported, not withheld

Creating a job returns what it will cost: wakes a day, tokens a day, and money where the model's price is known. A gated job reports what the wake that does fire will cost, since its ticks are a shell command and cost nothing.

Any cadence is allowed. The tool states what a frequent schedule costs; it does not refuse one, argue against one, or silently round it down. A floor was tried and removed (ADR-029). Transparency is the mechanism, not permission.

Listing answers questions about what is scheduled and what has already run. The job's own log holds every wake it has had, which is where "has this been running?" is answered — not from the conversation, which may have compacted since.

Acceptance criteria

  • create attaches the job to the current session when none is named.
  • create returns the job's identifier, its next wake, and its cost estimate.
  • A schedule resolving to a single instant creates a job that fires once.
  • A job created with a check reports the cost of a wake that acts, not a cost per tick.
  • edit changes only the fields given.
  • delete without an identifier, inside a wake, deletes the job that woke.
  • delete without an identifier and outside a wake is refused.
  • list reports each job's schedule, next wake, and recent runs.
  • No cadence is refused; the estimate is returned for every one.
  • Eval cases distinguish a request naming a time from one naming a condition.
